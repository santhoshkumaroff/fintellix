Non-Performing Asset definition. A loan or advance becomes a non-performing asset (NPA) when interest or instalment of principal remains overdue for a period of more than 90 days in respect of a term loan. An overdraft or cash credit account is treated as out of order if the outstanding balance remains continuously in excess of the sanctioned limit or drawing power for 90 days.

Special Mention Accounts. Before a loan turns into an NPA, banks identify incipient stress by classifying accounts as Special Mention Accounts. SMA-0 means principal or interest overdue between 1 and 30 days. SMA-1 means overdue between 31 and 60 days. SMA-2 means overdue between 61 and 90 days.

Asset classification. Non-performing assets are further classified into three categories. Substandard assets are those which have remained NPA for a period less than or equal to 12 months. Doubtful assets are those which have remained in the substandard category for a period of 12 months. Loss assets are those where loss has been identified but the amount has not been written off wholly.

Liquidity Coverage Ratio. The Liquidity Coverage Ratio requires banks to hold a stock of high quality liquid assets sufficient to cover total net cash outflows over the next 30 calendar days under a stress scenario. The ratio must be at least 100 percent.

Expected Credit Loss. Under the expected credit loss approach, the loss allowance is computed as the product of probability of default, loss given default and exposure at default. Stage 1 assets carry a 12-month expected credit loss. Stage 2 and Stage 3 assets carry a lifetime expected credit loss.

Maker-checker control. Every change to reported data must be prepared by one user (the maker) and approved by a different user (the checker) before submission. All changes are recorded in an audit trail with user, timestamp and old and new values.
